What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the area of computer science that deals with getting computers to do things we normally think only people can do. That covers stuff like understanding speech, spotting objects in a photo, translating between languages, or finding patterns hidden in huge piles of data.

What makes AI different from regular programming is that instead of a programmer spelling out every single rule, AI systems can pick up patterns from examples and get better as they see more data. That's also why AI needs so much data and computing power to work well.

Brief History of AI

The idea goes back to the 1950s, Alan Turing asked whether a machine could actually "think" and came up with a test for it. A few years later, in 1956, a group of researchers met at Dartmouth College and officially named the field "artificial intelligence," which is usually treated as the moment AI was born.

Progress wasn't a straight line though as there were stretches called "AI winters" where funding and interest dried up because the technology couldn't live up to the hype. Things really took off again in the 2000s and 2010s once computers got fast enough and there was finally enough data around to train on.

Types of AI

  • Narrow AI: Good at one specific thing, like recognizing faces or playing chess. This is basically all the AI we actually have right now.
  • General AI: The kind that could handle any task a human could. It doesn't exist yet and is still more of a goal than a reality.
  • Super AI: A hypothetical AI that would be smarter than humans at everything. Purely theoretical for now.
